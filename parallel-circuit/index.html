Formula for a parallel circuit
1 / RT = 1 / R₁ + 1 / R₂ + 1 / R₃
Setting any resistance to 0 Ω creates a short circuit across the power supply.
A branch is set to 0 Ω. In an ideal circuit, this creates a short circuit and the calculated current becomes unlimited. Real power supplies and circuits have internal resistance and protective devices.

Why does the equivalent resistance change?

In a parallel circuit, the branches provide several pathways for current. Reducing a branch resistance makes it easier for current to flow, so the equivalent resistance of the whole circuit becomes smaller.

Equivalent resistance describes how the complete circuit behaves from the perspective of the power supply. It is always less than the smallest individual branch resistance.
